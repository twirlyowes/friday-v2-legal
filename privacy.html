Privacy Policy

This policy covers F.R.I.D.A.Y, its Discord bot and its web dashboard. It explains the information those services process and the practical limits on deletion.

1. Information processed by the bot

Features vary by server settings. Depending on what a server enables and what users do, the bot may process:

  • Discord identifiers and profile details: user, server, channel and role IDs, usernames or display names, and avatar references needed for commands, tickets, logs and generated content.
  • Messages and attachments: direct messages sent to ModMail, ticket conversations, attachment links and file details, and message content examined by configured word/link filters. When enabled, filtered content may be copied to a server's moderation-log channel.
  • Moderation and verification records: warnings, strikes, reasons, timestamps, moderator identifiers, verification review records and related actions. The bot may also send moderation events to channels configured by the server.
  • Activity and utility data: staff activity totals and history, AFK reasons and timestamps, temporary voice-room ownership records, short-lived verification state, and text or profile details used to create quote images. The snipe feature temporarily caches up to 15 deleted messages per channel in process memory.
  • Server and owner configuration: command prefixes, selected channels and roles, ticket/verification/voice settings, moderation filters, YouTube notification settings, and server-access or no-prefix allowlists.
  • Operational information: error details and diagnostic logs needed to run and troubleshoot the service. If console mirroring is configured, some diagnostic output may also be sent to a Discord channel selected by the operator.

2. Information processed by the dashboard

  • Discord sign-in: when you sign in, Discord provides the dashboard with your user ID, username/display name, avatar reference, and the server list and permission information needed to determine which server settings you may access. OAuth access is requested for identity and server-list access; the dashboard checks current Discord membership and permissions again for protected actions.
  • Session cookie: the dashboard uses a first-party, HTTP-only session cookie to keep you signed in. The session is designed to last up to eight hours and may end earlier after a restart or session-store eviction. It is used for authentication, not advertising.
  • Settings and configuration history: changing settings can read or update Firestore records for server configuration, AutoMod, Anti-Nuke, AntiBot, command permissions and delegated-owner access. Configuration snapshots include saved settings and protection settings, the actor, timestamp and reason; the dashboard keeps the latest 30 snapshots per server and prunes older snapshots.
  • Audit history: successful configuration, protection, voice, ModMail, appeal, verification, message-publication and access-list actions may be logged with the acting user's ID and display name, time, action, changed setting names and, where relevant, a target user ID. The dashboard keeps up to 75 recent dashboard-audit events per server; no automatic time-based expiry is configured for these events.
  • Feature Center records: the dashboard may display or store ModMail conversation messages and staff replies; appeal reasons and case IDs; casebook staff notes; verification-review decisions and notes; staff activity data; temporary voice-room state; emergency-lockdown state; and message or embed content published through the dashboard, with its channel and creator details. Authorized server staff can access the relevant records through dashboard tools.
  • Abuse prevention: dashboard sign-in and API rate limits temporarily count requests using request metadata such as the client's IP address. These counters are operational safeguards, not an advertising profile.

3. Why information is used

Information is used to provide requested bot and dashboard features, route and review support tickets and appeals, maintain server settings, perform configured moderation and verification, manage voice rooms, calculate activity reports, enforce access permissions, publish messages, prevent abuse, diagnose failures and protect the service.

4. Storage and sharing

Selected configuration, moderation, access-control, ticket, appeal, case-note, activity and dashboard-history records are stored in Firebase/Cloud Firestore. The dashboard runs on a hosting service such as Render; its temporary web sessions and operational logs are handled by the deployment environment. The legal pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. Discord processes OAuth sign-in and hosts Discord messages, role changes, audit-log entries, ticket-log posts and transcript attachments sent through Discord.

Information may be visible to server owners and staff who have access to the relevant channels or dashboard controls. Service providers process data to operate their infrastructure. Information may also be disclosed where required by law or reasonably necessary to address security, abuse or harm. Each provider has its own privacy practices; see Discord's Privacy Policy.

F.R.I.D.A.Y is not designed to sell personal information.

5. Retention and deletion

There is no single retention period for all bot or dashboard data. Some records persist until an available control, a server administrator or the maintainer removes them.

  • ModMail: pending messages may remain stored if a user starts but does not finish the server-selection flow. Closing a ticket updates its metadata but does not automatically remove that record. A configured transcript may remain in the Discord ticket-log channel after the ticket channel is deleted. Dashboard ModMail threads and message history do not have a shared automatic expiry period.
  • Moderation, appeals and review records: warnings and strikes generally remain until removed using supported controls or by an authorized administrator. Appeal records, case notes and verification-review results may remain in Firestore; no common time-based expiry is configured for all of these records.
  • Activity data: the daily staff-activity reset zeroes current totals rather than deleting the underlying record. Historical activity snapshots may be retained separately.
  • Dashboard history: configuration history is capped at the latest 30 snapshots per server, and older snapshots are pruned when a new snapshot is captured. Dashboard audit history is capped at 75 events per server but has no configured time-based expiry.
  • Published messages: the dashboard can delete messages created and tracked through its message tool, including the tracked Firestore record. Copies, replies or other messages in Discord may remain independently.
  • Temporary in-memory state: pending captcha state is short-lived (the reviewed implementation uses a 60-second timeout); quote editing state expires after about an hour; and the snipe cache is bounded and clears when the process restarts. These limits do not delete copies already sent to Discord.
  • Configuration and access records: removing the bot from a server does not automatically erase Firestore configuration, delegated-owner/whitelist records, transcripts or provider logs. These may require separate action.
  • Dashboard sessions: a session is intended to expire after up to eight hours and is held in a temporary session store; a deployment restart can clear it sooner.

6. Your choices and requests

Server administrators can disable optional features, adjust channel permissions, use available commands to remove supported records, or remove the bot. Dashboard users can sign out to end their current session. These actions do not necessarily delete copies held by Discord, other providers or server staff.

For a privacy question or deletion request, use the maintainer's GitHub profile to request a private contact route. Do not post message contents, ticket transcripts, personal information or access tokens in a public issue. A request may need enough detail to identify the relevant server and feature, and it may not be possible to remove records from systems the maintainer does not control.

7. Security

The services use Discord permissions, server-side access checks, session protections and provider security controls. No internet service can guarantee perfect security. Server owners should restrict access to ticket, moderation and activity-log channels; operators should protect bot tokens and Firebase credentials.

8. Age and third-party services

Use F.R.I.D.A.Y only if you are eligible to use Discord under Discord's applicable age requirements and rules. The services rely on Discord, Firebase/Cloud Firestore, Render and GitHub Pages, and optional features may retrieve public YouTube feed data or send search terms to Wikipedia. Their availability and independent data practices are outside the maintainer's control.

9. Changes and contact

This policy may change as the code, deployment or data practices change. The effective date at the top identifies the latest published version. For general project information, visit the F.R.I.D.A.Y bot repository or the dashboard repository. For privacy requests, use the private-contact process in section 6.

F.R.I.D.A.Y is an independently maintained project and is not an official Discord product.
