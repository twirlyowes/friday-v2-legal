Privacy Policy

Effective date: 18 October 2026
Service: Pixel Villa Support Discord bot (“Bot” or “Service”)
Source repository: Pixel Villa Support on GitHub

Scope: This policy is a best-effort description based on publicly available source code and the Bot's known feature set. It is not a live infrastructure audit. The maintainer should verify deployed code, Firebase configuration, host logs, and Discord channel permissions and update this policy if they differ.

1. Information the Bot may process

Depending on which features are active, the Bot may process:

  • Discord identifiers and profile details: user, server, channel, and role IDs; usernames, display names, and avatar references used by commands, tickets, moderation output, or quote images.
  • Messages and commands: message content, command arguments, message/channel identifiers, timestamps, and author identifiers needed to respond to commands, run filters, manage tickets, or provide utilities.
  • Support tickets and DMs: messages and attachments sent to the Bot for support may be relayed to a configured server ticket channel. Depending on the ticket workflow, ticket content, attachment URLs, authors, and timestamps may be recorded in Discord channels or transcripts.
  • Moderation and warnings: user and server IDs, reasons, moderator details, timestamps, warning/strike records, and actions such as timeouts, kicks, or bans. Warning data may be held in local JSON files and/or selected records in Firestore, depending on the feature and deployed code.
  • Automod and message logs: messages may be checked against configured bad-word or link filters. When a filter or moderation feature logs an event, some message content and author/channel details may be copied to a configured Discord log channel.
  • Verification: verification attempts and temporary challenge/session data may be processed to verify members and assign configured Discord roles.
  • Staff activity: activity, voice participation, message counts, and selected moderation counts may be calculated for staff reports and stored in Firestore.
  • AFK and utility features: AFK reasons and timestamps, quote text and avatar references, and other data needed to respond to utility commands may be temporarily processed or retained according to the feature implementation.
  • Voice rooms and minigames: temporary voice-room ownership/channel identifiers and in-memory game state may be processed when those features are used.
  • Optional YouTube notifications: public feed/channel information and configured notification-channel details may be checked to post updates and avoid duplicate notifications.
  • Operational diagnostics: errors, status messages, and runtime diagnostics may be written to hosting logs. If console output is mirrored to Discord, that channel may also receive diagnostic context.

2. Why information is used

Information is used to run commands, provide support tickets, configure server features, moderate messages, track staff activity, verify members, manage voice rooms, run optional notifications, prevent abuse, and diagnose failures.

3. Storage and disclosure

The Bot uses Discord to deliver messages, role changes, logs, and ticket content. Selected settings and feature records use Firebase/Cloud Firestore. Some records, such as warnings, may also be stored in files within the running application. The hosting provider may process operational logs. The precise data held by each provider depends on the deployed code and configuration.

Server owners and staff with access to configured ticket, moderation, activity, and log channels may see information posted there. Discord and infrastructure providers process information under their own policies. See Discord's Privacy Policy and Firebase privacy information.

The maintainer does not intend to sell personal information.

4. Retention and deletion

Retention varies by feature and storage location. The Bot does not provide one guaranteed retention period for every category of data. Removing the Bot does not automatically remove messages already posted to Discord, ticket transcripts, local JSON files, Firestore records, hosting logs, or backups. Server administrators may need to delete Discord messages and records separately. Data stored in local files may persist while the host's persistent filesystem or deployment artifacts remain available.

Before publishing this policy, the maintainer should verify the actual retention behavior of the live deployment and set practical deletion periods for warnings, activity records, tickets/transcripts, Firestore documents, and provider logs.

5. Security

Reasonable care should be taken to restrict bot tokens, Firebase credentials, database access, and staff-only Discord channels. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and the Bot cannot guarantee absolute security. Never publish secrets or private ticket content in public issue trackers.

6. Children's privacy

The Bot is not designed to collect government IDs, payment information, or passwords. Users should not submit sensitive personal information through commands or support tickets. Discord's own minimum-age and eligibility rules apply.

7. Your choices and requests

You can stop using the Bot or ask the relevant server administrators to remove messages or records they control. For a request concerning information held by the Bot maintainer, use a private contact method provided by the maintainer; do not post personal information publicly. Requests may be subject to applicable law, technical limitations, and records that must be retained for legitimate reasons.

8. Third-party services

Discord, Firebase, hosting providers, and optional public data sources have their own privacy practices. This policy does not cover information those services collect independently of the Bot.

9. Changes

This policy may be updated when features, storage, or legal requirements change. The latest version will be published at this URL with an updated effective date.

10. Contact

For general questions, visit the GitHub repository. A private contact route for privacy requests should be added before public launch. Avoid submitting private user data or confidential ticket contents in public issues.
